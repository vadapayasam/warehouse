Game Feel

The invisible craft behind controls that feel right. From Steve Swink's framework to your app's micro-interactions.

Steve Swink, Game Feel (2009) · Applied to iOS and product design

What It Is

The tactile sense of manipulating a virtual object.

Game feel lives in the milliseconds between input and response. It is invisible in screenshots. It cannot be described. It must be felt. The same action performed with different timing, easing, or feedback can feel magical or broken.

This page breaks Swink's framework into seven testable principles, then lets you feel them directly through interactive demos.

The Framework

Seven Principles

Each principle is a lens for evaluating any interactive system. They apply to games, apps, and interfaces equally.

1

Predictable Results

Same input produces the expected outcome every time. Ambiguous press timing is the main failure source. If the user cannot form a mental model of cause and effect, feel collapses.

2

Instantaneous Response

A visible result within 70 to 100 ms. Past 100 ms the brain registers sluggishness. This is the hard threshold that separates crisp from muddy.

3

Easy but Deep

Low skill floor, high ceiling. Standard mappings win because they let users focus on mastery instead of learning. The best controls feel obvious on minute one and still reward expertise at hour one hundred.

4

Novelty

Subtle variation keeps repeated actions fresh. The enemy is identical linear animation every time. A button that wiggles slightly differently on each tap stays alive.

5

Appealing Response

Fun even in an empty space with no context. Invest in failure states. A jump that feels good when you miss the platform is better than one that only feels good when you succeed.

6

Organic Motion

Curved arcs, simulation-driven movement using velocity and forces. Never set position per frame. Easing curves and spring physics make motion feel alive instead of robotic.

7

Harmony

Every element supports one physical reality. Metrics stay consistent. If one button bounces and another snaps, the world fractures. Coherence across the entire surface matters more than any single interaction.

Techniques

Juice Toolkit

Reusable techniques from game development that apply directly to app and product design.

Technique What It Does App Equivalent
Coyote Time Allow an action a few frames after validity ends. 150 ms grace window. Pull-to-refresh that still triggers if released slightly late
Input Buffering Remember a press and fire when valid. Button that queues while animation completes
Hit-Stop Freeze for 40 to 80 ms on heavy impact. Success state that holds for a beat before dismissing
Screen Shake Few pixels, settles fast, needs a disable option. Subtle vibration on error or completion
Squash & Stretch Compress on land, stretch on leap. Biggest liveliness win. Button press depth, toggle spring animation
Particles / Flashes Burst and flash confirm contact. Confetti on achievement, glow on focus
Easing Never linear motion. Springs everywhere. All transitions, all the time
Sound Half the weight. Every action makes a sound. Haptics are mobile's sound channel
Secondary Motion Trails, dust. The world reacts to presence. Scroll parallax, list insert ripple
For Product Designers

Applying Game Feel to Apps

Every tap answers within 100 ms: visual, sound, and haptic together. Haptics are mobile's juice channel. Pair light, medium, and heavy feedback with action weight. Ease all UI motion. No linear tweens. Squash and stretch buttons, counters, toggles.

Respect Reduce Motion. Scale down shake and flash, but keep transitions short and meaningful. The goal is not spectacle. It is clarity through feedback.

The 30-Second Feel Test
Strip your app to one action. Do it for 30 seconds with no goal. If it does not feel good naked, the feel is not there yet.
