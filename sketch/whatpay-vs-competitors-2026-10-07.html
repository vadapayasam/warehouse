Own the payment people remember too late.

WhatPay vs Bobby, Rocket Money, Copilot Money, Monarch Money · 07 Oct 2026

A product comparison for the next release. WhatPay is not another all-in-one finance dashboard. Its sharper lane is the fixed payment: subscriptions, EMIs, trials and the dates attached to them.

WhatPay wins on control and fixed-payment clarity.

It is strongest for people who want to enter their own payments, avoid bank connections, track EMIs beside subscriptions, and pay once for the full tool. It is weaker when the job is automatic discovery, cancellation, household finance, or net worth.

Recommended laneFixed payments first. Finance platform later, if research proves the need.

The category splits in two directions.

Bobby is the closest substitute. Rocket Money, Copilot and Monarch compete for the larger job of managing money, not only remembering recurring charges.

Read the competitors by job, not by feature count.

Closest direct substitute

Bobby proves that manual tracking can be a product, not a compromise.

Its pitch is simple: add the fixed costs yourself, see them at a glance, and get a due notification. WhatPay needs a sharper reason to switch: EMIs, trials, widgets, and a better first-run path. Do not copy a broad finance dashboard. Beat the small tracker at the payment moments it ignores.

Opportunity

Make “manual” feel fast.

Templates, currency setup, one-screen date entry and useful defaults are not admin. They are the speed layer that lets WhatPay keep its privacy posture.

Rocket Money

Pressure from automation.

Rocket Money owns the “find it and take action” story: linked accounts, recurring charge detection, cancellation, bill negotiation, budgeting, credit and net worth. It is the strongest reason a user might ask why manual entry is worth the effort.

  • Learn: explain the first value before asking for setup.
  • Do not copy: bank linking or service-heavy cancellation before WhatPay has product-market evidence.

Sources: App Store · rocketmoney.com

Copilot

Pressure from polish.

Copilot makes automated finance feel calm and considered across Apple devices. Its subscription detection is a supporting feature inside a much larger money view.

Learn: show the whole payment rhythm, not just a list.

Pressure from scope.

Monarch turns recurring payments into one part of a shared financial home with budgets, goals, reports and collaboration.

Learn: use a clear recurring calendar. Do not copy: household complexity by default.

Three moves make the wedge legible.

These are product moves, not a new roadmap. Each one makes the current promise easier to understand before adding a larger finance surface.

01

Own EMI and trial clarity.

Show payment type, remaining instalments, next date and trial expiry as first-class states. This is where WhatPay has a meaningful difference from subscription-only trackers.

02

Make manual entry instant.

Lead with popular services, sensible dates, remembered currency and a clear “Add” path. The user should reach a useful list before they feel they are filling a form.

03

Turn one-time payment into trust.

Make the lifetime purchase part of the product story: no recurring fee for the tracker, no bank credentials needed, and no service pretending to act on the user’s behalf.

Validate before expanding scope.

  • Do users understand “EMI” in the first five seconds?
  • Do people prefer manual entry when setup takes under one minute?
  • Does the one-time purchase feel more credible after a real reminder fires?
  • Are widgets used as a daily glance or only during setup?

The App Store story should sell the moment before the charge.

WhatPay already has a visual advantage: an Apple-native, colorful fixed-payment list. The screenshot set should make the job obvious without pretending to be a full finance platform.

04
Show the EMI state.

Use remaining instalments and the next due date as the visual proof that WhatPay is more than a subscription list.

05
Show the widget.

Make the value survive outside the app. A glanceable widget is a clearer differentiator than another chart.

06
Use direct copy.

Recommended first screenshot line: “Know what you pay next.” Subline: “Subscriptions, EMIs and trials in one private list.”

Recommended positioning

WhatPay is the private, Apple-native tracker for every recurring payment that has a date.

Next three experiments
  1. Test the “subscriptions, EMIs and trials” promise against “know what you pay next.”
  2. Measure time from launch to first saved payment with templates.
  3. Compare widget usage after a reminder versus after onboarding.
